03 / FIELD NOTES

Behind the movement.

Observations from the intersection
of motion, mechanics, and making.

001

Start with the joint, not the creature.

RIGGING＋

A convincing mechanical creature starts with a single believable connection. Before building a full leg, define its range of motion: where can the joint rotate, where does it stop, and what drives it?

Block out one limb with simple shapes. Test its extremes before adding detail. A mechanism that reads clearly in silhouette gives every later layer of the design something solid to build on.

002

Let the gears do the math.

PROCEDURAL＋

Two meshing gears share a contact speed, not a rotation speed. Their tooth counts determine the ratio: a 20-tooth driver turns a 40-tooth follower at half its speed, in the opposite direction.

Drive the follower from the input rotation with θ₂ = −θ₁ × N₁ / N₂. Keep one source of motion and let the relationships carry it through the system. This makes timing changes easier and keeps the mechanism coherent.

003

Precision still needs personality.

MOTION＋

Mechanical accuracy tells the viewer what a system does. Timing tells them how it feels. A brief pause before engagement can suggest weight, resistance, or a machine gathering energy.

Keep the constraints believable, then explore acceleration, settling, and tiny delays between connected actions. Personality often lives in the transition between rest and movement.
